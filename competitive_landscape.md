# Competitive Landscape

I looked at the websites of Freedom, Cold Turkey, Tiimo and Routinery. I haven't used any of them for long, so this is based on what they say about themselves (Routinery's site blocked me, so for it I used its App Store page).

## Quick comparison

| | Focus Bear | Freedom | Cold Turkey | Tiimo | Routinery |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Main idea | Blocking + routines + breaks | Blocking | Strict blocking | Visual planner | Routine timer |
| Platforms | Mac, Win, iOS, Android | Mac, Win, iOS, Android, Linux, Chromebook | Win, Mac | iOS, Android, Watch, web | iOS, Android, Watch, Mac |
| Blocks apps/sites | Yes | Yes | Yes (strictest) | No | No |
| Routines | Yes | No | No | Yes | Yes |
| Price | 7-day trial, then subscription | Free / $39.99 per year / $199 lifetime | One-time purchase | Free + Pro | Free + subscription |

## What makes Focus Bear different from these apps?

The others each do one thing. Freedom and Cold Turkey block distractions, Tiimo and Routinery help you plan or follow a routine. Focus Bear puts blocking, step-by-step routines and movement breaks in one app, and it's clearly built for people with ADHD and autism, not just "anyone who wants to be productive". That focus shows in the details, like guiding you one step at a time so you don't have to decide what to do next.

## If I were a user, why would I choose Focus Bear over competitors?

If my problem was both "I get distracted" and "I can't keep a routine", I'd pick Focus Bear because I'd otherwise need two apps, for example Freedom plus Routinery, and they wouldn't talk to each other. I'd also like that it works on all my devices at once. If I only needed to block sites on my laptop, though, I'd probably go with Cold Turkey, since it's a one-time payment and very strict.

## What's one feature that other apps have that Focus Bear doesn't?

Tiimo has a visual timeline of the whole day with color-coded tasks and widgets. From what I read, Focus Bear guides you through routines step by step, but I didn't see a big-picture view of the day like that. Freedom also has focus music and Pomodoro sessions built in, and it supports Linux.

## Based on my research, what's one improvement I think Focus Bear could make?

A visual day view, similar to Tiimo's timeline, so people can see their routines, breaks and focus sessions in one place. For people who find planning hard, seeing the day at a glance could make it feel less overwhelming. I'd want to try the app properly first (issue #8) to check whether something like this already exists.
