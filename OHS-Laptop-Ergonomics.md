# OHS: Laptop Ergonomics

## Research & Learn

**Risks of using a laptop without an external monitor or keyboard**
The screen and keyboard are attached, so one of them is always in the wrong place. Looking down at the screen strains the neck and shoulders; raising the laptop to eye level puts the keyboard too high and strains the wrists and arms. Over time this can cause neck and back pain, wrist discomfort and headaches.

**Equipment that improves posture**
- An external monitor, or a laptop stand to raise the screen.
- An external keyboard and mouse.
- A proper chair with back support and adjustable height.
- A footrest, if feet don't reach the floor comfortably.

**Adjustments for a healthier workspace**
- Monitor: top of the screen at or just below eye level, about an arm's length away.
- Chair: feet flat on the floor, knees around 90 degrees, back supported.
- Desk: elbows about 90 degrees, wrists straight when typing, shoulders relaxed.

**Daily habits that reduce the impact of long laptop use**
- Take a short movement break roughly every 30–60 minutes: stand, stretch, walk.
- Follow the 20-20-20 rule for the eyes: every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet (about 6 meters) away for 20 seconds.
- Check posture regularly, and drink water.
- Keep the screen brightness and room lighting comfortable.
