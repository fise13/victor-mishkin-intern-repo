# Professionalism Reflection

## Research & Learn

**Professional vs. unprofessional behaviour in a casual workplace**
- Professional: replying within a reasonable time, showing up on time, doing what I said I'd do, telling people early if something is delayed, joking in a way that includes everyone.
- Unprofessional: disappearing without a word, blaming others, gossiping, jokes at someone's expense, ignoring messages, pretending to work.

Casual doesn't mean careless. Laughing and having fun is welcome, as long as people are respected and work gets done.

**Respectful communication in remote teams**
- Remember that tone is hard to read in text: be clear, add context and avoid sarcasm.
- Disagree about the idea, not the person.
- Respond to feedback by asking questions, not by getting defensive.
- Say when I need something and by when.

**Giving and receiving feedback professionally**
Focus on actions and facts, not on the person. Assume good intent. Ask for clarification instead of reacting right away. When receiving feedback, listen first, ask for examples, then decide what to change.

## Reflection

**Great teamwork I've experienced**
I worked on several pilots as a QA tester for AutoCore, testing the product with real people. It worked very well: we got really good feedback and a full understanding of what the user wants. It was effective because we focused on real users and listened to them instead of guessing.

**How can I make my communication clear, respectful and inclusive?**
- Say what I need and when, in short sentences, one request per message.
- Avoid sarcasm and unclear hints, and explain abbreviations or jargon.
- Give people time to reply, since not everyone answers instantly or works the same way.
- Read my message once before sending to check that it isn't too sharp.

**How can I help create a positive, fun and professional environment?**
With humor and stories from work, while making sure the jokes are friendly and include everyone, and by being supportive when someone is stuck.

**How can I prepare effectively for meetings with supervisors and colleagues?**
- Technical setup: good lighting, a quiet place, and nobody behind me in the camera.
- Warm up my speaking: sometimes I read aloud in English, loudly and clearly, so I'm comfortable speaking.
- Before the meeting I write down what I did, what I'm stuck on, and my questions.

**What would it look like to be proactive during my internship?**
Not waiting to be told what to do: when I finish a task, I say so and ask what's next or suggest something. When something is unclear, I ask early instead of guessing. I share problems and ideas as soon as I notice them, not at the last minute.

**How would I follow up with someone who owes me information or action, and escalate if there's no response?**
1. Write a clear message in Discord with what I need and by when.
2. If there's no answer after about 2 working days, send a short, friendly reminder.
3. If it still blocks me after another day or two, ask my supervisor for help and explain what I already tried.

If it's urgent or blocking my work, I escalate sooner. I always keep it polite and factual.

**Things I won't do**
- Sleep during working hours.
- Pretend to be busy: if I have no task or I'm stuck, I say so and ask for work or help instead of faking activity.
